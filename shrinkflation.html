Shrinkflation in Singapore's private homes¶

DAO2702 group assignment, poster source code.

Question. Did Singapore's private homes shrink to keep the price tag within reach, and is that still happening in 2025?

Data.

  • properties2025.csv: URA private residential transactions for 2025 (course dataset).
  • properties23.csv: the same fields for Aug 2020 to Dec 2023 (Lecture 6 dataset), used for the trend.
  • ura_ppi_quarterly.csv: URA Private Residential Property Price Index, 1Q2009 = 100 (data.gov.sg).
  • hh_income_change.csv: SingStat yearly change in median monthly household employment income, resident employed households (data.gov.sg).
  • Rules used as parameters: MAS TDSR 55% with a 4% stress-test rate, 75% LTV; URA dwelling-unit caps (GFA / 70 m² from 2012, GFA / 85 m² from 2019).

The nine charts (each a different chart type), in poster order:

# Question Chart
1 Are 2025 new launches smaller than resale homes? Back-to-back histogram
2 When did homes shrink? Line with interquartile band
3 Which sizes disappeared? 100% stacked bar
4 Is it just location? Coefficient plot
5 Did the shrink hold the price tag? Scatter with constant-price curves
6 How do developers price a launch? Violin
7 Why then? Prices against incomes Diverging lollipop
8 How much home can a household borrow for? Sensitivity heat map
9 What happens once shrinking is capped? Waterfall

Run top to bottom. Figures land in figs/.

4. Coefficient plot: is the shrink just location?¶

OLS of ln(floor area) on vintage era (base 1990–2008). The controlled model adds district, property type (apartment or condominium) and transaction-year effects. Standard errors are clustered by project, since units in one project are designed together.

In [5]:
pdf = lease.select("area", "era", "district", "type", "year", "project").to_pandas()
pdf["lna"] = np.log(pdf["area"])
pdf["era"] = pdf["era"].astype(str)
ref = f"C(era, Treatment('{ERAS[0]}'))"
m_raw = smf.ols(f"lna ~ {ref}", data=pdf).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": pdf["project"]})
m_adj = smf.ols(f"lna ~ {ref} + C(district) + C(type) + C(year)", data=pdf).fit(cov_type="cluster", cov_kwds={"groups": pdf["project"]})
coef = []
for name, m in [("raw", m_raw), ("controlled", m_adj)]:
    for e in ERAS[1:]:
        k = f"{ref}[T.{e}]"
        lo, hi = m.conf_int().loc[k]
        coef.append((name, e, np.exp(m.params[k]) - 1, np.exp(lo) - 1, np.exp(hi) - 1))
        print(f"{name:<11} {e}: {coef[-1][2]:+.1%} [{coef[-1][3]:+.1%}, {coef[-1][4]:+.1%}]")
print(f"projects (clusters): {pdf['project'].nunique()}, R² raw {m_raw.rsquared:.2f}, controlled {m_adj.rsquared:.2f}")

8. Sensitivity heat map: how much home a household can borrow for¶

Largest loan allowed by MAS: monthly instalment ≤ 55% of gross income (TDSR), computed at max(loan rate, 4%) over 30 years. With a 75% loan-to-value cap the price ceiling is loan / 0.75. Affordable floor area = price ceiling / PSF. Cash and CPF top-ups beyond the 25% down payment are ignored. Because banks must use at least 4%, rate cuts below 4% do not change the table.

9. Waterfall: once shrinking is capped, the price tag rises¶

New-sale transactions, Aug–Dec 2020 (the first months in the Lecture 6 file) against 2025. Uses geometric means, so ln(price) = ln(PSF) + ln(area) splits the change exactly. Each segment (CCR/RCR/OCR) is compared with itself and weighted by its 2025 share, so a shift in where launches happen does not count as a price change.

seg = (
    df.filter(pl.col("sale") == "New Sale", pl.col("year").is_in([2020, 2025]))
    .group_by("segment", "year")
    .agg(n=pl.len(), lp=pl.col("price").log().mean(), lpsf=pl.col("psf").log().mean(), la=pl.col("area").log().mean())
    .sort("segment", "year")
)
wide = seg.pivot(on="year", index="segment", values=["n", "lp", "lpsf", "la"])
wide = wide.with_columns(w=pl.col("n_2025") / pl.col("n_2025").sum())

d_area = (wide["w"] * (wide["la_2025"] - wide["la_2020"])).sum()
